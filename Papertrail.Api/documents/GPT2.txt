Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners (GPT-2)

Abstract:
Natural language processing tasks are typically approached with task-specific architectures and datasets.
We demonstrate that language models begin learning these tasks without any explicit supervision when
trained on a new dataset of Internet text called WebText. Our largest model, GPT-2, transfers well to most
tasks and significantly outperforms task-specific architectures. We argue that a language model trained on
sufficiently large and diverse text will naturally learn to perform a wide range of tasks.

Introduction:
The ability of language models to learn useful representations and perform well on downstream tasks has
improved significantly with scale. However, most existing language models are trained on limited domain data
and evaluated only on specific downstream tasks.

We present evidence that very large language models trained on diverse, large-scale, unlabeled text corpora
develop broad linguistic knowledge and can be applied to diverse tasks with minimal architectural changes
and no task-specific fine-tuning.

WebText Dataset:
We created a large, diverse text dataset called WebText by scraping web pages linked from Reddit. Reddit
users have already curated interesting and relevant links, so we leverage this as a proxy for importance. We
collected all outbound links from Reddit posts that received at least 3 karma with text documents extracted.
The resulting WebText dataset contains text from 45 million web pages, totaling 40 GB of text.

Model Architecture:
GPT-2 uses the same model architecture as GPT, which is a Transformer with 12 layers, 768 hidden dimensions,
and 3072 dimensions in the feed-forward layers. The largest model (GPT-2) has 1.5 billion parameters and
48 layers of Transformers.

Task Learning Without Explicit Supervision:
We observe that language models, when trained on sufficiently large corpora, naturally learn to perform a
wide range of downstream tasks without any explicit supervision. This includes:

1. Machine translation - learns to translate text with only monolingual training
2. Question answering - learns to answer questions despite not being trained on QA datasets
3. Summarization - learns to summarize articles and passages
4. Semantic similarity - learns to rank similar documents

Zero-shot Transfer:
GPT-2 demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer learning performance on various tasks. Unlike fine-tuning
approaches that require task-specific datasets, GPT-2 can perform tasks by simply conditioning on task
descriptions in natural language.

For example, for machine translation, we simply prepend "translate to French:" to the English text, and the
model learns to produce French translations. The model achieves 23.5 BLEU on WMT-14 English-French
without any training data or task-specific architectures.

Training and Scaling:
The models were trained using mixed precision, gradient checkpointing, and distributed training across
multiple GPUs. Larger models were trained for 10 billion tokens of text. The larger the model and more
diverse the training data, the better the transfer performance.

Results:
- Question Answering (SQuAD): 66 F1 score (vs 90+ for fine-tuned models)
- Machine Translation: 23.5 BLEU (WMT-14 En-Fr)
- Summarization: 20 ROUGE (vs 34 for fine-tuned models)
- Natural Language Inference: 55% accuracy

Implications:
The success of GPT-2 suggests that the scale of language models and the diversity of training data are more
important factors for language understanding than task-specific architectural innovations. This challenges
the conventional wisdom that different tasks require different model architectures.

Conclusion:
We have shown that large language models trained on diverse internet text learn general linguistic knowledge
that transfers to many downstream tasks without any explicit supervision. This suggests that large-scale
language modeling should be considered as a potential component of various NLP systems.
