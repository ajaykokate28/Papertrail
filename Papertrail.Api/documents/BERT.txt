BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding

Abstract:
We introduce BERT, a new method of pre-training language representations. Unlike recent language
representation models, BERT is designed to pre-train deep bidirectional representations from unlabeled text
by jointly conditioning on both left and right context in all layers. As a result, the pre-trained BERT
model can be fine-tuned with just one additional output layer to create state-of-the-art models for a wide
range of tasks, such as question answering and language inference, without substantial task-specific
architecture modifications.

Introduction:
Language model pre-training has been shown to be effective for improving many natural language processing
tasks. These include sentence classification tasks, and token-level tasks such as named entity recognition and
question answering. There are two existing strategies for applying pre-trained language representations to
downstream tasks: feature-based and fine-tuning based.

The feature-based approach, such as ELMo, uses task-specific architectures that include the pre-trained
representations as additional features. The fine-tuning approach, such as the Generative Pre-trained
Transformer (OpenAI GPT), introduces minimal task-specific parameters, and is trained on downstream
tasks by simply fine-tuning all pre-trained parameters.

Bidirectional Training:
A critical limitation of standard language models is that they are unidirectional, and this limits the choice of
architectures that can be used during pre-training. For example, in OpenAI GPT, the authors use a
left-to-right architecture, where every token can only attend to previous tokens in the self-attention layers.

In contrast, BERT uses a masked language model (MLM) pre-training objective that enables the pre-training
of bidirectional representations. The MLM randomly masks some of the tokens from the input, and the
objective is to predict the original vocabulary id of the masked word based only on its context.

Masked Language Modeling (MLM):
The masked language modeling task is inspired by the Cloze task in which the reader predicts masked words
based on context. We randomly select 15% of the tokens in each sequence. For the tokens that were selected,
we perform the following procedure:
1. With 80% probability, replace the token with [MASK]
2. With 10% probability, replace the token with a random token
3. With 10% probability, keep the token unchanged

The final hidden vectors corresponding to the mask tokens are fed into an output softmax over the vocabulary.

Next Sentence Prediction (NSP):
Many important downstream tasks such as Question Answering (QA) and Natural Language Inference (NLI)
are based on understanding the relationship between two sentences. The NSP task is modeled as a binary
classification task. When choosing the sentences A and B for each pre-training example, 50% of the time B is
the actual next sentence that follows A, and 50% of the time it is a random sentence from the corpus.

The NSP task is important because sentence relationships are not directly captured by language modeling.
However, the implications of this task are still being studied and debated by the community.

Representation and Embedding:
BERT uses learned token embeddings with a special [CLS] token at the beginning of every input sequence.
The final hidden state corresponding to this token is used as the aggregate representation of the entire
sequence for classification tasks.

For each token, its input representation is constructed by summing the corresponding token, segment, and
position embeddings. Segment embeddings distinguish between sentence A and sentence B.

Fine-tuning:
To use BERT for a downstream task, we simply plug in the task-specific inputs and outputs into BERT and
fine-tune all the parameters end-to-end. The main difference from pre-training is the output layer. At the
input, sentence A and sentence B from pre-training are analogous to sentence pairs in downstream tasks.

Results:
BERT achieves state-of-the-art results on a broad set of NLP tasks. On the GLUE benchmark, BERT obtains
a 80.4% average score, surpassing the previous state of the art by 5.1%. On the SQuAD question answering
dataset, BERT achieves 93.2% and 95.1% on Exact Match and F1 score, respectively, compared to the
previous best published results.

Conclusion:
We have demonstrated that the use of bidirectional pre-training is crucial for producing powerful language
representations. Unlike Vaswani et al. and Devlin et al., BERT uses a masked language model pre-training
objective instead of a left-to-right language modeling objective. This is the main contribution of BERT.
